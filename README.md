## Bugs Mode, Rival Mode, and hunt web (2026-10-20)

The Android game and menus use landscape orientation. The main menu offers **Bugs Mode** and **Rival Mode**. Bugs Mode keeps its 20-strand starting web and four large visible bugs, but stretches the same layout across a larger, scrollable arena. The blue spider starts at the lower end and one red rival starts at the upper end. Both chase bugs; the player wins by catching more than the rival before the bugs are gone or the timer expires. Escaped bugs count toward ending the round. The blue spider and red rival keep their starting size in this mode. Bugs can cut links, and the player can cut or repair them. The right-side item bar can be hidden, and Pause stops play and opens a Main Menu option. A tapped bug struggles and cuts silk faster as the blue spider approaches; the other bugs keep their normal timing. Rival Mode now uses the same Bugs Mode web geometry and arena size at each level. Its bugs fly in and stick to the web, while bug targets and player power-ups differ. Rival Mode keeps its expanding rival roster. The separate Web Trap challenge keeps its existing web and ladybugs. `tests/test_bugs_mode.gd` and `tests/test_rival_levels.gd` cover these modes.

In Rival Mode, spiders start at the same visual size as Bugs Mode and show their eaten bug count above them at every level. Tapping a strand moves the player along it; cutting requires selecting Cut Web first. Levels 1–5 retain their growth rules. After Level 5, caught bugs raise the count without increasing spider size. A spider can eat another spider only with a higher bug count; ties do not cause an elimination. The level ends when the player reaches the bug target, a rival reaches it, or every rival is defeated. Defeated rivals do not respawn in these count battles. The rival walking sound plays while any rival moves, and the rival voice plays only when a rival has more bugs than the player.

## Play

Open this folder (`spider_wars`) in Godot 4.7.2 and run the project. A portrait **Spider Territory** splash appears first; it advances automatically after 2.8 seconds or immediately on tap. Then the main menu opens; tap **Play** to start **First Hunt, Level 1** (`res://scenes/first_hunt.tscn`). Every First Hunt level starts with the web already built and several small and large ladybugs scattered across it. The web extends left, right, up, and down within a visible arena boundary; spiders cannot cross that boundary, and the camera follows the player inside it. Level 1 has one rival and a short camera reveal. The blue spider begins at the lower edge. Levels 1–5 keep one rival; later levels add more. Tap a bug or a rival to chase it along the web. Tap any web junction to travel there; the spider eats bugs it encounters and then continues to the selected junction. Arrow keys also move the spider on desktop. Most bugs stay on the web until eaten; every third First Hunt bug can break free and fly away after a short wait, and replacements fly in over time. Eating increases each spider's size. Larger rivals hunt and eat smaller rivals, gaining at least one growth point and half the defeated rival's bug count. When a rival becomes larger than the player, gameplay pauses, the camera focuses on that rival, and a warning appears before the chase resumes. The player's bug-count target is five in Level 1 and rises by five per level. Eating a smaller rival adds half of its current bug count, rounded down, to the player's count; defeated rivals stay gone through levels 1–5. A larger rival eating the player ends the run with a **Game Over** popup and **Restart Level** button. **Only reaching the player's bug target completes a First Hunt level.** Completing it clears any bugs still visible. **Next: Level** reloads First Hunt with a higher target and the player's current size.

The game uses a portrait mobile layout. The 1080×1920 interface scales and centers within tall or narrow phone viewports, keeping the power buttons and result popup on screen. The world fills any extra space, and the camera clamp adjusts to the visible viewport size and zoom. The top counters follow the screen top, and power icons sit at the left middle. Use two fingers to pinch zoom on mobile or the mouse wheel on desktop.

In First Hunt, **Cut Trail** or tapping a strand spends a cut charge to break a link; **Create Web** spends a build charge to restore or add one. A strand holding a bug cannot be cut until the bug is eaten. **Speed Boost** makes the blue spider faster for three seconds, and **Freeze Rival** freezes active rivals for three seconds with an animated ice shell. Only the player uses these powers; rivals hunt bugs and smaller spiders without power-ups. Each player power has limited charges. Level 1 introduces the rival with a short camera move before movement starts.

Each bug the player eats gives **2 coins**; a level win gives **10 coins**. Star coins fly from the catch or result popup toward the coin balance. Level completion and Game Over use distinct garden victory and failure textures with gold stars. The Web Trap challenge uses the victory and failure result textures. Open **Spider Shop** to buy and select a spider. **Classic** is free, **Swift** costs 30 coins and moves 25% faster, **Shade** costs 45 coins and can become invisible for four seconds, and **Hunter** costs 60 coins and catches bugs from farther away. **Giant** costs 75 coins and starts Level 1 with two extra growth points. Purchases, coin balance, and the selected spider are stored in `user://spider_wars_progress.cfg` between sessions. Headless `--script` tests disable save writes.

In the separate **The Web Trap** challenge, the blue spider weaves an irregular web that continues above the starting screen. All six bugs are ladybugs: four start in the lower web and two are farther up. They begin glued to web junctions and visibly struggle. Strong ladybugs tear free sooner; the others loosen their glue over time. Once free, they crawl along connected strands toward the edge and may escape if neither spider catches them. The camera follows the blue spider upward while the controls and score stay on screen. Both spiders step with their legs, face their travel direction, and stop before colliding with each other on a strand. Ladybugs have different visible sizes. Catching one stops the spider while it repeatedly bites; an eating progress ring shows the time remaining, and larger ladybugs take longer. The score increases when eating finishes, with a colored particle burst. A red AI spider follows the same web and uses the same eating rules. Tap a strand to cut it. A spider already on that strand retreats to its junction, and the red spider must reach an endpoint and weave the strand again before it can use that path. The player can block its approach. Cutting a strand carrying a ladybug lets it escape. The round ends when every ladybug has been caught or escaped; the spider with more catches wins, with a draw for equal scores. **Play Again** restarts the round. More bug types can be added later.

Tap a ladybug to send the blue spider along the shortest available web route, or tap a junction to travel to it while eating bugs encountered. Arrow-key movement cancels that route. Each round starts with **3 cut charges** and **2 build charges** for the player. Tap an active strand away from a junction to cut it, or switch on **Cut Trail** so the spider cuts each link just after crossing it; both spend one cut charge per link. Tap **Weave Link**, then tap a broken strand to send the spider to an endpoint and rebuild it. To add a new link, tap **Weave Link** and select two nearby junctions. A new link must not cross existing silk. If a selected ladybug can only be reached through a broken link, the spider rebuilds that link automatically when a build charge is available. The red spider has 2 rebuild charges. The buttons show remaining player charges, and **Cancel Path** stops automatic navigation. On a keyboard, C toggles Cut Trail, B toggles Weave Link, and Escape cancels the route.

## Verification

Use Godot 4.7.2 from this folder. On Windows, the GUI-subsystem executable may return control to PowerShell before its test output appears. This command waits for each process, captures its output, and reports the exit code:

```powershell
$godot = 'C:\Users\Supreme Traders\Downloads\Godot_Engine\Godot_v4.7.2-stable_win64.exe'
foreach ($name in @('test_core', 'test_tutorial_flow', 'test_first_hunt', 'test_rival_levels', 'test_early_rival_clear', 'test_rival_power_unlocks', 'test_mobile_layout', 'test_result_buttons', 'test_bug_web_cut', 'test_bug_flight', 'test_splash_screen', 'test_menu_flow', 'test_startup_playability', 'test_face_to_face', 'test_player_input', 'test_web_trap', 'test_web_powers')) {
    $stdout = Join-Path $env:TEMP "spider_$name.stdout.log"
    $stderr = Join-Path $env:TEMP "spider_$name.stderr.log"
    $process = Start-Process -FilePath $godot -ArgumentList @('--headless', '--path', '.', '--script', "res://tests/$name.gd") -PassThru -WindowStyle Hidden -RedirectStandardOutput $stdout -RedirectStandardError $stderr
    if (-not $process.WaitForExit(15000)) { $process.Kill(); $process.WaitForExit() }
    Write-Output "$name exit $($process.ExitCode)"
    Get-Content $stdout
    Get-Content $stderr
}
```
